# DD Call Simulation — Prime Lee's Summit (Institutional JV Raise)

> Simulation — a plausible adversarial reading, not a prediction.
> Grounded ONLY in supplied wiki facts (entity page, 2026-08-11). Where a number
> cannot be reconciled from what was given, the analyst notices — that IS the finding.
> Context: institutional JV equity raise for the second-stage capital raise (after
> Stonehaven Estates). Analyst: partner-track VP at a regional institutional JV fund.

## Transcript

**Analyst:** Thanks for the time. Let's start with the asset. Five five-storey multifamily buildings, roughly 610 residents, Price Chopper anchored, two retail strips. How many of the tenants shown on the concept renderings are under executed lease?

**Sponsor:** *(pause)* None are executed yet. We're in active LOI conversations with the anchor and two national tenants.

*Analyst's note: There it is. The rendering shows a full tenant roster; the pipeline is zero signed. That's not fatal — grocery-anchored pre-development raises run like this — but it decides how I underwrite the retail layers. He said it openly, which is good. The bad version is finding out at closing.*

**Analyst:** Understood, and that's normal at this stage. Walk me through the capital stack then: layer by layer, what's the source and what's the risk it's bearing?

**Sponsor:** Six layers. Predevelopment and sponsor equity takes the entitlement risk. Land and entitlement JV takes us from acquisition to shovel-ready. Horizontal infrastructure — roads, utilities, stormwater — then multifamily construction on senior debt plus JV equity, retail construction on retail debt plus JV equity, and permanent financing refinances the stabilized components.

*Analyst's note: The layering logic is sound — it matches each source to the risk it best underwrites. What I'm not hearing: the split. Who puts in what, at what preferred return, and where does the promote start? A stack with no numbers is a diagram.*

**Analyst:** The layering reads well. What's the equity split on the land JV, and what preferred return are you quoting this layer?

**Sponsor:** We're finalizing the term sheet — I'd rather share final numbers than preliminary ones.

*Analyst's note: Fair answer, wrong room. Every fund hears "finalizing the term sheet." I need the range. "We're targeting X to Y" costs nothing and tells me they understand their own stack.*

**Analyst:** Fine — give me the range you're underwriting internally. And while you're at it: what does the sponsor's check look like? How much predevelopment equity is actually in, in cash?

**Sponsor:** Sponsor equity covers site control and entitlement. I can share the dollar figure in the data room.

*Analyst's note: Second non-answer on money. I have asset facts and no sponsor facts. The Stonehaven raise was the first — so this is a young sponsor asking institutions to fund the second deal. Skin-in-the-game is the single most important data point for that profile, and it's not in the materials.*

**Analyst:** We'll pull the data room. Let's test the anchor economics instead. Price Chopper as grocery anchor on a highway-291 corridor at 33,000 cars a day — what rent per square foot, what term, and what's their contribution to the retail construction layer?

**Sponsor:** Grocery-anchored retail in the corridor trades at strong yields. We've underwritten the anchor at market terms.

*Analyst's note: "Market terms" is not a term sheet. The anchor drives the whole retail underwrite — cap rate, construction debt, exit. If the anchor is underwritten at market assumptions with no executed LOI, the retail layer of the stack is carrying a pricing assumption I can't verify.*

**Analyst:** Let me try a reconciliation. You said the multifamily layer runs on senior debt plus JV equity. If the buildings deliver with 610 residents across 300-odd units, what's the stabilized yield-on-cost you're underwriting? And what cap rate supports the permanent-financing layer?

**Sponsor:** Our proforma shows the multifamily component stabilizing comfortably above the market for the corridor.

*Analyst's note: Third answer without a number. "Comfortably above market" could mean 50 basis points or 300. There's no proforma in the data room index, no NOI bridge, no hard-cost number, no yield-on-cost. I cannot size the deal.*

**Analyst:** I'll be direct: I have zero numbers in front of me. No proforma, no cost estimate, no lease pipeline, no equity contribution, no term sheet range. What am I supposed to underwrite?

**Sponsor:** The asset speaks for itself — highway 291 frontage, 33,000 cars a day, 800+ parking, 25 EV stalls, a resort pool, a gym. This is a flagship development site.

*Analyst's note: The asset facts are good. But when the pitch falls back on amenities, the sponsor is buying time on the economics. The amenities are the cherry; I underwrite the cake. Also: tenant names on renderings are not commitments — the wiki's own diligence rule. He knows this. Everything is pipeline, nothing is executed.*

**Analyst:** The site is genuinely strong — I'll grant you that. Grocery-anchored, 33k cars, KC metro growth corridor. Now the question behind the question: Stonehaven Estates was your first raise. What did it close, and what's the sponsor's track record on delivering the underwrite?

**Sponsor:** Stonehaven closed successfully and is our proof of concept. We've structured this raise to be simpler than what we're building now.

*Analyst's note: "Closed successfully" without a size, an IRR, or a delivery-vs-underwrite comparison. The second raise on the back of a first raise needs the first raise's numbers on the table. Absence reads as either thin or unprepared.*

**Analyst:** One more on the structure. Master development entity, component LLCs for each parcel — multifamily, grocery/retail, pharmacy, restaurant, common infrastructure. Who holds the JV investor's interest, what's the waterfall, and who controls the component LLCs' decisions?

**Sponsor:** The master entity holds the structure and investors participate at that level. Component LLCs isolate construction risk. Counsel is reviewing the full legal architecture.

*Analyst's note: "Counsel is reviewing" — expected at predevelopment. But investors participating at master level with risk isolated in components they don't control is a governance question, not a legal formality. Control rights and information rights at component level need to be explicit. Also the securities-law flag: this structure requires counsel/tax/lender review, and the memo will note it.*

**Analyst:** Let's say we pass on this round. What's the milestone that makes you worth a second look at construction phase?

**Sponsor:** Executed anchor LOI and the land JV term sheet. We're targeting both this quarter.

*Analyst's note: Two concrete milestones, one quarter. That's the first specific commitment I've heard all call. It's also the honest bar: this deal becomes underwritable the moment the anchor is signed and the land JV economics are real.*

## The Internal Memo

**To:** Partner / Investment Committee
**From:** VP, Real Assets
**Re:** Prime Lee's Summit — $X million JV raise (sponsor: Veritas Development Group)

**Recommendation: PROCEED-WITH-REPS — revisit at anchor LOI + term sheet.**

The real reasons, in order:

1. **The asset is real.** Grocery-anchored, 33k cars/day on Highway 291, KC metro growth corridor, layered capital stack that matches sources to risk. This is a well-conceived development. The site does not need rescuing.

2. **The presentation is not fund-ready.** Three requests for numbers (equity split, preferred return range, sponsor contribution) produced three non-answers. No proforma, no hard-cost, no yield-on-cost, no NOI bridge, no lease pipeline. An institutional JV fund cannot underwrite a flagship raise on amenity description alone.

3. **The tenant gap is the material risk.** The renderings present a full roster; the pipeline is zero executed. Our diligence rule for this asset is written on their own wiki: names on renderings are not commitments. Until the anchor LOI is executed, the retail layers of the stack are carrying pricing assumptions.

4. **Sponsor history is thin and unquantified.** Stonehaven "closed successfully" with no size, IRR, or delivery-vs-underwrite. For a second raise, the first raise's numbers must be on the table. Absence is read as either thin or unprepared — either way it compresses terms.

5. **Governance needs explicit drafting.** Master-level participation with component-level risk isolation needs control and information rights spelled out, plus the securities/counsel review done *before* marketing, not concurrent with it.

**What changes the answer:** executed anchor LOI (or a term sheet with named national tenants), a term-sheet range for the land JV layer, sponsor cash equity disclosed, and a proforma the analyst can reconcile. None of these are unreasonable asks; all of them are the difference between a real raise and a discovery process at the IC.

**The reconciliation pack to build before the real call** — the 5 numbers that must tie:
1. Hard cost per door (multifamily) → total construction cost
2. Yield-on-cost (stabilized NOI ÷ total cost) → vs. corridor comps
3. Stabilized cap rate on retail/grocery components → vs. exit comps
4. Anchor rent PSF × term → vs. the retail debt service it must support
5. Equity split + pref + promote per layer → vs. sponsor cash actually deployed
