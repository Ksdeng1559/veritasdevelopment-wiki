## Part 1 — What is Build-to-Rent (BTR)?

**Definition:** Build-to-Rent is a housing development model where homes (single-family, townhomes, or multifamily) are **purpose-built and owned by a single investor/operator as long-term rental assets** — not sold to individual buyers.

### BTR vs. Build-to-Sell (BTS) — the structural difference

| Dimension | Build-to-Sell (BTS) | Build-to-Rent (BTR) |
|---|---|---|
| **End state** | Units sold to individual buyers | Units held as rental portfolio |
| **Revenue model** | One-time sale proceeds | Recurring rental income + appreciation |
| **Capital recovery** | At sale | Over 10–30 years of operations |
| **Developer exposure** | Exits at sale; no ops | Holds asset; needs property mgmt capability |
| **Tenant** | N/A (sale closes) | Long-term renters (institutional-grade design) |
| **Investor type** | Homebuilders, individual buyers | Institutional investors, REITs, family offices |
| **Construction design** | Buyer-finished (granite, premium) | Tenant-durable (high-wear finishes, energy-efficient) |
| **Financing** | Pre-sales can gauge demand | Must secure full financing upfront |

### Why BTR is attractive to investors (why Seth Wilson is searching for it)

1. **Recurring revenue** — rent rolls instead of lump-sum exits
2. **Institutional scale** — single asset owner with 50–500+ unit communities
3. **Lower maintenance per unit** — new construction vs. value-add rehabs
4. **Tax efficiency** — depreciation + cost segregation
5. **Demand tailwind** — millennials + boomers both want renting flexibility
6. **Middle-market gap** — underserved between SFR (scattered) and large multifamily (200+ units)
7. **Predictable exit** — sell stabilized portfolio to REIT or institution in 5–10 years

### Why BTR is attractive to operators (why Veritas should care)

1. **Repeatable revenue** — development fee + site-prep + ongoing construction pipeline
2. **Long-term client relationships** — BTR operators build portfolios, not one-offs
3. **Site-prep + excavation depth = competitive moat** — most BTR operators don't own site work
4. **Geography match** — KC metro is a top-10 BTR market nationally
5. **Lower sales risk** — no absorption uncertainty; rents pre-leased before completion

### KC BTR landscape (active operators)

- **Avital Homes** — KC metro BTR general contractor, single-family + multifamily, builds institutional-grade rental communities
- **Summit Homes KC** — Lee's Summit active in 10+ communities
- **Local multifamily GCs** — several boutique builders serving the institutional-BTR pipeline

### Project highlights

**Oldham Village East ($416M)** — Two apartment buildings: 320 units + 250 units. Costco-anchored. First-wave development of a larger mixed-use vision.

**East Village ($481M)** — Three-phase project. Includes Costco + apartments + townhomes + commercial. **$111M in tax incentives requested.** Drake Development is the lead.

**Legacy Ridge (505 units)** — Master-planned community on 131 acres owned by an LDS church entity. Griffin Riley Property Group revised plans in response to community feedback (dropped apartments from an earlier version). Includes 73,504 sqft of neighborhood retail.

**Arborside ($1.5B+ / 1,000 acres)** — Massive scale; sandwiched between I-470 and Lake Jacomo. Conceptual plan only — full buildout mix TBD. Will be a multi-decade build.

### Market context

- **Submarket strength:** Lee's Summit is consistently called the **#1 multifamily submarket in KC metro** (per Legendary Apartment Brokers)
- **Demand drivers:** KC metro proximity, major highway access, robust local economy
- **Rent growth:** +6.4% YTD (per Jackson County RMI report)
- **Absorption:** 174 new-construction communities, 171 homes sold/mo (per NewHomeSource data)
