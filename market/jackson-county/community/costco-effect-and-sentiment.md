# Lee's Summit — Public Sentiment & Community Friction Profile

> **Source:** LS Economic Development Outline 2026 + LSEDC Investor Benefits 2026
> **Use:** Sentiment-aware strategy · community engagement playbook · brand positioning

## Public Sentiment Snapshot

> **"Public sentiment mining indicates a highly positive baseline toward master planning, though friction surfaces regarding dense residential zoning and municipal execution pacing."**

The LSEDC's own analysis frames Lee's Summit as **pro-development with specific friction zones**.

---

## Primary Growth Catalysts (Positive Sentiment)

### 1. The "Costco Effect"

> **"The relocation and opening of the new Lee's Summit Costco Warehouse at the SE corner of US 50 and MO 291 represents the primary narrative driving localized civic excitement."**

**Why it works:**
- Costco is a **prestige retail anchor** (highest sales tax generator in retail)
- Residents strongly favor **preventing sales tax leakage** to neighboring jurisdictions
- Visible, scheduled development with regular progress updates
- Reinforces LS as a "destination" rather than a "pass-through"

**Veritas takeaway:** For any project to gain local support, **anchor with a retailer or amenity that prevents spending leakage**. Costco, Discovery Park, and Oldham Village all have this.

### 2. Premium Visual Assets

> **"Highly visible progress updates (such as developer reels on Instagram and grading tracking videos on Facebook) for Discovery Park and Oldham Village maintain high public alignment with corporate growth."**

**Why it works:**
- **Real-time progress content** on social media builds familiarity
- Behind-the-scenes access (grading, equipment, crews) creates **sense of ownership**
- Developers who communicate visually get rewarded with sentiment

**Veritas takeaway:** **Document the build aggressively** — Instagram reels of grading and site work, Facebook updates, YouTube time-lapse. This is free marketing that builds community support.

### 3. Workforce Development Wins

- Missouri Innovation Campus (MIC) recognition
- $3B+ mega-projects creating local jobs
- 80% of workers commute <24 miles (local-hire support)

## Strategic Friction Points (Negative Sentiment)

### 1. Developer Incentive Scrutiny

> **"High-value tax abatements (such as the modified $408M mass timber initiative) draw systematic critiques regarding public subsidy allocation and the structural shifting of tax burdens away from corporate developers."**

**The pattern:**
- TIF/Chapter 353/Chapter 100 incentives are publicly visible
- Acts of Council include the abatement terms
- Media coverage highlights the dollar value of forgone tax
- Public asks: *"What are we getting in return?"*

**Veritas takeaway:** Frame **direct community benefit** in every incentive application:
- Created jobs (number, type, salary)
- Infrastructure improvements (with $ values)
- Public amenity contributions
- Tax base growth

### 2. Density vs. Infrastructure Strain

> **"Residential expansions, specifically high-density senior housing complexes and rental properties, meet localized resistance via formal protest petitions. Civic worry centers on traffic bottlenecks along key arteries and overcrowding in the R-7 school district."**

**The pattern:**
- High-density residential (especially senior + rental) draws protest petitions
- Concerns about traffic bottleneck
- Concerns about school overcrowding
- Public meetings become contentious

**Veritas takeaway:** **Pre-empt the friction**:
- Engage buffer-zone neighbors (185-ft radius) before formal submittal
- Provide traffic impact studies + mitigation plan
- Coordinate with R-7 district on capacity projections
- Lead with retail anchor (where support is strongest)

### 3. Municipal Execution Pacing

> *"Municipal execution pacing"* (per LSEDC's own analysis)

Residents want infrastructure upgrades to **keep pace with new development**. When they don't, sentiment turns.

**Veritas takeaway:** Use TIF/Chapter 353 funds to directly fund infrastructure improvements (roads, utilities, drainage). This is both **incentive-eligible** and **sentiment-positive**.

## Veritas Community Engagement Playbook

### Pre-Application (Days 1-30)

1. **Identify buffer-zone neighbors** within 185-ft radius
2. **Door-to-door outreach** — face-to-face > email > mail
3. **Capture supporter list** — often 60-70% of neighbors are neutral-positive
4. **Address concerns directly** — traffic, drainage, school

### Application Phase (Days 30-90)

5. **Traffic impact study** — proactively, not as a condition
6. **Drainage analysis** — Lee's Summit has had flood concerns
7. **School capacity commitment** — coordinate with R-7
8. **Anchor with retail/amenity** — convenience store, cafe, park

### Approval Phase (Days 90-180)

9. **Visual progress content** — Instagram, Facebook, YouTube
10. **Construction-job local-hire** — 80% commute <24 miles, prioritize local
11. **Public Q&A sessions** — monthly, in the neighborhood
12. **Address naysayers directly** — public meetings, not just behind-closed-doors

### Construction Phase (Year 1-2)

13. **Weekly progress videos** — short reels (60-90 sec)
14. **Quarterly site tours** for neighbors
15. **Prompt issue response** — drainage, dust, noise, traffic

### Completion Phase (Year 2+)

16. **Open-house / community event** before opening
17. **Document the build story** — full case study
18. **First-tenant celebration** — invite neighborhood

## Veritas Strategic Recommendation

### The expensive lesson from the Senior CCRC pattern

**13 protest petitions** on the senior community project required **site revisions** — a delay that affects every project thereafter. The lesson is:

> **Don't design projects in isolation. Design them in conversation with the community.**

The pre-application engagement (door-to-door, traffic studies, drainage, anchor retail) is **not optional**. It's the **cheapest insurance** against entitlement risk.
