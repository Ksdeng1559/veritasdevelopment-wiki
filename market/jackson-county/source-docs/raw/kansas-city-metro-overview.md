# Kansas City Over View.docx

Kansas City has evolved from a “hidden gem” into one of the premier destinations for institutional and private capital in the United States.

While coastal and Sun Belt metros face oversupply and volatility, the Kansas City Metropolitan Area delivers a rare combination of robust macroeconomic growth, central geographical importance, and highly favorable yield dynamics.

According to data from the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City, the regional Commercial Real Estate (CRE) Index rose sharply from 0.21 to 0.52, confirming a substantial acceleration in development activity and capital inflow.

Commercial Development: The Midwest's Logistics & Tech Engine

Kansas City is the 15th-largest industrial market in the nation, spanning over

345 million square feet

of space. Institutional investors are heavily financing new construction due to several structural pillars:

#### Unrivaled Connectivity:

As a centralized intermodal hub, businesses operating out of Kansas City can reach

85% to 90% of the U.S. population within a two-day truck drive

. It features four intersecting interstate systems (I-35, I-70, I-29, I-49) and stands as one of the largest rail hubs by tonnage in the country.

#### Exceptional Vacancy & Absorption:

Even amid national real estate cooling, KC recorded nearly

1.93 million square feet of positive net absorption

through the first half of the year. Industrial vacancy dropped to a remarkably tight

, ranking KC fourth lowest in aggregate vacancy among the top 30 U.S. industrial markets.

#### Mega-Project Magnets:

Industrial demand is anchored by massive corporate build-to-suit commitments. Major drivers include the

$4 billion Panasonic electric vehicle battery plant

in De Soto (creating 4,000 permanent jobs), a

3.72 million square foot industrial development

by Block Real Estate Services, and major tech/data center footprint expansions by Google, Meta, and Amazon.

#### The Build-to-Rent (BTR) Boom:

Corporate and bulk institutional buyers now control over

20% of the single-family housing inventory

in the area. Institutional capital has heavily pivoted toward BTR townhomes to capture the "missing middle"—appealing to millennials and families who demand neighborhood space but are priced out of single-family home ownership due to high mortgage rates.

#### Urban Core Redesign:

Downtown Kansas City has become the fastest-growing residential neighborhood in the region. It acts as a massive economic engine, housing 32% of the city’s jobs and converted more than 50 historic office buildings into apartments. A projected

33% population growth over the next decade

will add roughly 11,000 residents to the downtown core.

#### Defensive Yield Insulated from Oversupply:

Major Sun Belt markets (like Phoenix, Austin, and Atlanta) have seen compressed yields due to a massive wave of apartment oversupply. In contrast, Kansas City's under-construction pipeline sits at a highly conservative

3.3% of total inventory

, shielding investors from sudden vacancy shocks.

#### Superior Capital Cap Rates:

While coastal premium properties struggle to net positive spreads, KC offers highly attractive yield-oriented profiles. Capitalization rates for workforce housing and standard commercial assets average between

6.36% and 7.44%

, ensuring steady passive returns.

#### The Global Stage Catalyst:

Institutional money thrives on infrastructure momentum. Millions of dollars are pouring into civic megaprojects, including the KPKC Stadium (the world's first dedicated professional women's sports stadium) and a

$1 billion mixed-use neighborhood transformation

along the Berkeley Riverfront. This rapid, high-profile modernization has permanently put Kansas City on the map for international real estate portfolios.
