## The 20-Year Framework

Lee's Summit is managing a **historic expansion** with **$3.5B+ in total developments actively approved or breaking ground**. The city operates under a 20-year master framework driven by:

- **Ignite! Comprehensive Plan** (city master plan)
- **Lee's Summit Economic Development Council (LSEDC)** (public-private partnership)
- **City Council + Mayor** (governance)

### Strategic Pillars

> *"...prioritizing commercial anchors, mixed-use infrastructure, and high-tech corporate parks."*

The city is transitioning from a **bedroom community** into a **primary regional employment hub**.

## 4,000-Acre Master Corridor Expansion

> **"Massive strategic deployment of vacant church-owned tracts into integrated high-density master-planned community nodes."**

This is the single largest land-use plan in Lee's Summit's modern history. Translation:

- Church-owned land (LDS, Methodist, etc.) is being converted into high-density mixed-use communities
- The **Arborside** (1,000 acres, $1.5B+) and **Land Reserve - North Lee's Summit** (1,036 acres) are the flagship projects
- Multi-decade buildout — Veritas is well-positioned as a site-prep + development execution partner

## Land Allocation & Targeted Master Nodes

### Activity Centers & Mixed-Use Place Types
- Standard arterial zones being transitioned into **"Urban Mixed-Use"** and **"Neighborhood Commercial"** zones
- Walkable density is the explicit goal
- This favors ground-up developers who can deliver integrated mixed-use product

### Employment Core Isolation
- Transportation corridors along **I-470** and **US Route 50** being reserved for:
  - High-tech manufacturing
  - Medical distribution
  - Warehousing logistics
- Veritas's site-prep + excavation depth is a direct fit for these employment nodes

## Capital Expenditure Allocations (Projected)

To support a **population targeted to exceed 125,000 within the horizon**, capital funding is structured to **aggressively frontload roadway networks and recreational utilities** to keep pace with structural growth.

| Cost category | Priority |
|---|---|
| Roadway networks | **HIGH** (frontloaded) |
| Recreational utilities | **HIGH** (frontloaded) |
| Other infrastructure | Phase-in |

## Targeted Outcomes (From ED Incentive Policy)

The City is seeking investment that supports:

1. **Development & maintenance of infrastructure** — bridges, streets, signals, stormwater, water/sewer
2. **Attracts/retains targeted businesses** — professional services, science/tech, healthcare, manufacturing, arts, entrepreneurship
3. **Preserves/enhances residential developments** — quality design, mixed types, accessibility
4. **Encourages business retention/expansion** — partnership opportunities
5. **Generates positive community image** — meets/exceeds UDO design requirements

## Major Active Mega-Projects ($2.5B+ Confirmed)

| Project | Capital | Footprint | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Discovery Park** | $900M | 3.9M sq ft campus | Medical tech, luxury multifamily, Class-A office, hospitality |
| **East Village & Oldham Village** | $481-492M | Mixed-use | Townhomes, retail, **160K sq ft Costco** |
| **The Arbor Development** | $1.5B | 1,000 acres | Multi-phase master-planned community |
| **Pryor Ridge / NW Pryor** | $408M | Phase-based | Mass timber commercial + ~200 luxury housing |

## Why This Matters for Veritas

### 20-year horizon suggests partnership-of-decade opportunity

- $3.5B+ active development pipeline with more in planning
- **Veritas as construction partner** for site-prep + excavation across multiple projects
- **Veritas as JV partner** for owned development (the 4,000-acre corridor expansion may include land available for JDG-equity participation)

### Timing considerations

- **Near-term (2026-2028):** Active projects break ground — Veritas site-prep ready
- **Mid-term (2028-2032):** Arborside + Land Reserve NLS phases 1-2
- **Long-term (2032-2046):** Phases 3+, infrastructure expansion, employment cores

## Risk Factors for the 20-Year Plan

1. **Community friction** — protest petitions on senior + density projects
2. **Infrastructure pacing** — possible delay if frontloaded capex isn't enough
3. **Interest rate environment** — high-value tax abatement scrutiny
4. **School capacity** — R-7 crowding mentioned in sentiment reports
5. **General friction: cost shifting** — public skepticism about TIF/Chapter 353 use

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Frame Veritas as a long-term partner** — not project-by-project
2. **Engage LSEDC at Patron-level** — direct leadership access
3. **Identify Phase 1 land in 4,000-acre corridor** — first-mover advantage
4. **Stack statutory incentives** — TIF + Chapter 100 + Chapter 353
5. **Lead with infrastructure narrative** — community sentiment rewards this
