# Strategic Development Framework: Industrial Expansion in North Lee’s Summit

## 1. Regional Industrial Market Dynamics and Demand Analysis

As of the first quarter of 2026 (1Q26), the Kansas City industrial market is characterized by exceptional resilience and high-velocity leasing activity, reinforcing its status as a premier centralized intermodal hub. For the North Lee’s Summit expansion, regional performance serves as the critical strategic foundation; with national markets cooling, Kansas City’s sustained positive absorption and declining vacancy necessitate the immediate activation of the Land Reserve to capture the next wave of capital investment.

The following market drivers dictate the current regional strategy:

\*   \*\*Exceptional Vacancy Compression\*\*
    \*   \*Strategic Implication:\* The 4.5% vacancy rate—the third lowest among the top 30 U.S. industrial markets—requires the North Lee's Summit Land Reserve to deliver immediate inventory to prevent "tenant flight" to competing regional submarkets.
\*   \*\*Record Rent Trajectories\*\*
    \*   \*Strategic Implication:\* Average asking rents have climbed 36.3% over the past seven years, now exceeding $6.20/SF. This high-rent floor justifies the intensive public-private infrastructure investments required to prepare the site for high-value Class A occupiers.
\*   \*\*Sustained Net Absorption\*\*
    \*   \*Strategic Implication:\* The region recorded 1.9 million square feet (MSF) of net absorption in 1Q26 alone. North Lee’s Summit must secure its position within the intermodal hub by providing scale that can accommodate the large-footprint users driving these totals.
\*   \*\*Sector Diversification (Data Center Suppliers)\*\*
    \*   \*Strategic Implication:\* With data center suppliers forecasted to consume an increasing share of regional space in 2026, the Land Reserve must ensure zoning and utility readiness for specialized manufacturing and technical support industries.

### Key Regional Performance Indicators (1Q26)

| Metric | Current Value (1Q26) | Forecasted Trend (Remainder of 2026) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Vacancy Rate\*\* | 4.5% | Decreasing toward 4.2% |
| \*\*Net Absorption\*\* | 1.9 MSF (Quarterly) | Steady/Positive (3.2 MSF past 4 qtrs) |
| \*\*Average Asking Rent\*\* | $6.20/SF - $6.35/SF | Increasing/Above pre-pandemic levels |
| \*\*Construction Pipeline\*\* | 6.9 MSF | Shifting from BTS-heavy to Spec-heavy |

A fundamental shift is occurring as the market transitions from Build-to-Suit (BTS) dominance to speculative development. While 56% of the current 6.9 MSF pipeline remains BTS, developers are increasingly breaking ground on speculative projects to meet demand for modern Class A space. This "spec-heavy" environment necessitates that North Lee’s Summit maintain "shovel-ready" status to compete with regional spec projects like VanTrust’s Building IV at Raymore Commerce Center. This regional demand for immediate, flexible capacity directly dictates the land use strategy for the North Lee’s Summit Land Reserve.

## 2. North Lee’s Summit Land Use Allocation and Phasing

The 1,036-acre North Lee's Summit Land Reserve is a generational asset requiring a meticulous, phased multi-use approach. This strategy ensures that industrial growth is integrated with residential, commercial, and recreational infrastructure, creating a sustainable ecosystem rather than a monolithic industrial park.

### Light Industrial Development Pipeline by Phase

| Phase | Total Industrial Acreage | Projected Building Space (SF) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Phase 1 | 72 Acres | 1,092,000 SF |
| Phase 2 | 217 Acres | 2,587,000 SF |
| Phase 3 | 0 Acres (Focus on Mixed-Use/Town Center) | 0 SF |
| \*\*Total\*\* | \*\*289 Acres\*\* | \*\*3,679,000 SF\*\* |

The projected 3,679,000 square feet of Light Industrial space will establish North Lee’s Summit as a regional economic engine. The critical "So What?" of this framework is its integration: by allocating 109 acres to Mixed-Use zones and 88 acres to a Town Center, the City supports the industrial workforce with immediate access to 1.6 million square feet of retail and 302,000 square feet of medical/office space. This synergy fosters a "live-work-play" environment that enhances the attraction of high-skilled tenants.

To support this massive footprint, the framework identifies the following primary infrastructure requirements and land-use constraints:

\*   \*\*Water and Sewer:\*\* Requires proposed sanitary gravity mains, force mains, and the implementation of a specific \*\*Lift Station\*\* to manage high-capacity industrial waste.
\*   \*\*Power and Connectivity:\*\* Features KCPL overhead power, an 80-foot electric transmission easement, and a robust AT&T fiber/copper network to support data-intensive operations.
\*   \*\*Multi-modal Connectivity:\*\* A planned trail network connecting to Lake Jacomo, designed to provide non-vehicular access for the workforce.
\*   \*\*Aviation Constraints:\*\* Development must strictly adhere to the \*\*Aviation Easement (RPZ)\*\* identified in the site utilities plan, which limits structural heights and land use in designated flight paths.

This physical site capacity is engineered to align with the City’s specific economic development policies, transitioning from land-use planning to policy fulfillment.

## 3. Strategic Alignment with Lee’s Summit Economic Development Policy

Growth in North Lee’s Summit is governed by the "Ignite! Strategic Plan," a vision focused on "ensuring fiscal sustainability" through regulated industrial expansion. This policy ensures that development contributes to the general welfare and maintains the City's ability to deliver high-quality services.

### Targeted Outcomes for Development

The City prioritizes projects that achieve the following:
1.  \*\*Infrastructure Expansion:\*\* Seeking "opportunities where the expansion of public infrastructure may create new economic development areas," particularly through the creation of new streets, bridges, and sanitary sewer facilities.
2.  \*\*Attraction of Targeted Businesses:\*\* Prioritizing firms that leverage a "residential base comprised of highly educated and highly skilled individuals," specifically in science, technology, and manufacturing.
3.  \*\*High-Quality Job Creation:\*\* Projects must contribute to the "attraction and retention of high quality jobs" that support family households through "good income."

### Developer Checklist: Industrial Consideration Criteria

Prospective developers must satisfy the following ten core criteria for incentive consideration:

\*   \[ \] \*\*Level of Abatement:\*\* Requesting no more than 50% of real or personal property taxes.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Duration of Abatement:\*\* Abatement lasts no longer than needed to induce the project and matches regional historical standards.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Blight Clearance and Public Benefits:\*\* Project reduces or eliminates undesirable or blighted conditions.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Tax Stabilization:\*\* Project immediately generates direct or indirect new tax revenues (sales, property, or franchise taxes).
\*   \[ \] \*\*Sustainable / Green Development:\*\* Incorporation of sustainable or green development or renewable or energy-saving practices.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Statement of Need (“But For Test”):\*\* Evidence that the project would not be undertaken without the requested incentive.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Size of Project and Total Investment:\*\* Redevelopment projects should invest at least $5 million.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Company Equity:\*\* At least 15% of total project costs funded from company equity, not private bank loans.
\*   \[ \] \*\*New Job Creation:\*\* Creation of jobs not relocated from elsewhere in the City.
\*   \[ \] \*\*Job Retention:\*\* Proof that incentives will retain jobs that would otherwise leave the City for another jurisdiction.

The \*\*I-470 North Targeted Planning Area\*\* is the cornerstone of this policy. The 1,100 acres owned by Property Reserve, Inc. is a priority due to its massive \*\*scale\*\*—it is the last large-acreage tract available for a master-planned vision. While development is recognized as challenging due to "topography" and the need for significant "sewer and traffic improvements," its potential for fiscal impact makes it the City Council's primary focus.

## 4. Incentive Framework and Financial Tool-Kit

Lee’s Summit utilizes its incentive toolkit to offset "extraordinary public infrastructure costs," ensuring the City remains competitive with other municipalities on the Missouri side of the Kansas City metro area.

### Availability of Incentives Matrix

| Tool | Minimum Investment | Typical Incentive Value/Cap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Tax Increment Financing (TIF)\*\* | $5M New / $3M Existing | Up to 25% of total private costs |
| \*\*Chapter 100 (Bonds)\*\* | $5M New / $3M Existing | 50% Abatement for 10 Years |
| \*\*Chapter 353 (Redevelopment)\*\* | $5M New / $3M Existing | 50% Abatement for 10 Years |
| \*\*Comm. Improvement Dist. (CID)\*\* | Public Improvement Required | Up to 1 cent sales tax OR Special Assessment |
| \*\*Trans. Development Dist. (TDD)\*\* | Public Improvement Required | Up to 1 cent sales tax OR Special Assessment |
| \*\*Land Clearance Authority (LCRA)\*\* | $500,000 | 50% Abatement for 10 Years |
| \*\*Sales Tax Reimbursement\*\* | $250,000 | Up to 1/2 cent sales tax |

\*\*Chapter 100\*\* and \*\*Chapter 353\*\* programs are the "most favorable" structures for industrial projects, offering a standard 50% tax abatement for 10 years. This term is strategically aligned with historical packages in the region to attract capital-intensive manufacturing and data center suppliers. 

\*\*Special Taxing Districts\*\* (CID, TDD, and NID) are essential for funding the infrastructure identified in the site utilities plan. These districts allow for special assessments or captured sales tax to pay for "public-use improvements"—such as the proposed lift station, bridges, and signalization—that confer a benefit on the property and the general public. These tools ensure that the burden of massive site preparation is shared, paving the way for high-quality workforce integration.

## 5. Workforce Integration and Economic Impact Criteria

The North Lee’s Summit strategy is validated by the regional labor market, where a 3.9% unemployment rate (50 basis points below the national average) signals significant economic strength. This tight market allows the City to be selective, prioritizing "educated and highly skilled" residents and rejecting low-wage industrial models.

To ensure long-term community value, the framework mandates that industrial projects provide \*\*average compensation that meets or exceeds the average pay for Jackson County\*\*. This ensures that the 3.68 million square feet of industrial space supports robust family households and the City’s high standard of living.

Recruitment efforts are guided by growth trajectories in key \*\*Industrial-Using Employment Sectors\*\*:
\*   \*\*Construction:\*\* Up 9.2% (The regional leader, supporting the current development surge).
\*   \*\*Manufacturing:\*\* Down 1.9% (A targeted sector for rebound through modern Class A provision).
\*   \*\*Trade/Transportation/Utilities:\*\* Down 3.8% (Targeted for stabilization via the region’s intermodal hub status).

The final Strategic Vision for North Lee’s Summit is one of calculated equilibrium. By delivering 3.68 million square feet of Light Industrial space supported by Chapter 100 incentives and phased infrastructure, the City achieves its goal of "fiscal sustainability." This framework ensures the tax base grows while the City continues to provide an unmatched quality of life to both residents and businesses.

North Lee’s Summit stands as the premier, policy-aligned destination for regional industrial investment, offering the scale, infrastructure, and strategic incentives necessary for global success.
