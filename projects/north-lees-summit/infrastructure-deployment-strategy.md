# Multi-Phase Infrastructure Deployment Strategy: North Lee’s Summit Land Reserve

## 1. Executive Strategy & Site-Wide Infrastructure Baseline

The development of the 1,036-acre North Lee’s Summit Land Reserve is the primary vehicle for achieving the City’s "Ignite!" strategic goals. As lead consultant, I have determined that a synchronized utility rollout is the only viable method to mitigate developer risk and ensure the site remains competitive. Infrastructure is the catalyst; without front-loaded, high-capacity utility trunks, the 1,036-acre site remains static. This roadmap transitions the Reserve from a "targeted planning area" to an active development engine by aligning physical deployment with the City Council’s infrastructure-first mandate.

### Baseline Utility Audit
Current site conditions, derived from ground-truth geospatial data \[SOURCE\_IMAGE\_6\], establish the following utility framework:

\*   \*\*Energy & Communications:\*\*
    \*   An \*\*80’ Electric Transmission Easement\*\* bisects the property. This is a Tier-1 asset capable of supporting the high-density loads required in later phases.
    \*   Existing \*\*KCPL Power lines\*\* and \*\*AT&T Copper/Fiber\*\* (overhead and buried) provide a baseline for initial construction trailers and low-intensity early-phase needs.
\*   \*\*Hydraulics:\*\*
    \*   The site is served by existing \*\*Lee’s Summit Water mains\*\* and secondary "Other" water mains at the periphery.
\*   \*\*Sanitary:\*\*
    \*   Existing inventory includes \*\*Sanitary Gravity Mains\*\* and \*\*Force Mains\*\* that require immediate expansion to support industrial-scale output.

### Geospatial Constraints Analysis
Deployment must navigate the following "no-build" or "restricted-build" zones:

\*   \*\*Aviation Easement (RPZ):\*\* Located at the north-central boundary \[SOURCE\_IMAGE\_6\]. This easement strictly prohibits high-occupancy uses and restricts building heights. Consequently, high-density "Town Center" (Phase 3) elements must be strategically shifted southward, away from this flight path.
\*   \*\*Abandoned Spire Gas Line:\*\* This legacy asset must be accounted for during grading. Direct civil teams to prioritize either full remediation or clear marking to prevent conflict with the proposed north-south utility trunks.

### Strategic Alignment
The \*\*I-470 North Targeted Planning Area\*\* designation requires that this roadmap satisfy City Council "General Policies." By establishing a synchronized backbone, we ensure the site meets the City’s mandate for "Strategic Economic Development" and "Fiscal Sustainability."

Phase 1 activates the high-yield industrial and commercial sectors. The strategic priority is the establishment of the \*\*Southern Lift Station\*\*. This is not merely a utility requirement; it is a financial necessity. Per Policy Statement 11, this "extraordinary public infrastructure" provides a general public benefit, serving as the linchpin to unlock the \*\*$5M minimum investment threshold\*\* required for TIF eligibility \[SOURCE\_IMAGE\_9\].

### Utility Deployment Roadmap
Direct civil engineers to execute the following commands:
\*   \*\*Sanitary Support:\*\* Prioritize the immediate installation of \*\*Proposed Sanitary Mains\*\* connecting the Phase 1 industrial footprint to the Southern Lift Station. This must be sized to handle the combined load of the 1M+ SF industrial and 136k SF medical components.
\*   \*\*Water Distribution:\*\* Direct the extension of the \*\*12” Proposed Water Main\*\* along the central corridor. This is a critical path item to meet fire-flow requirements for the 611,000 SF retail zone.

### The "So What?" Layer
The southern lift station represents the primary "extraordinary cost" that justifies the use of Tax Increment Financing. By documenting this as a regional benefit, we ensure the project qualifies for "most favorable consideration" under the City’s Incentive Policy.

## 3. Phase 2: Scaled Residential and Industrial Expansion (544 Acres)

Phase 2 is the "growth engine," shifting toward a massive \*\*R-1 (Single-Family)\*\* residential footprint. Unlike the higher-density R-2 found in Phase 1, the \*\*206 acres of R-1 residential\*\* require a significantly more sprawling lateral grid. 

### Infrastructure Scaling Analysis
\*   \*\*Industrial Load:\*\* Supporting an additional \*\*2,587,000 SF\*\* of Light Industrial space. Civil teams must size the main trunk lines in Phase 1 with the capacity for this doubling of industrial footprint.
\*   \*\*Residential Grid:\*\* Integrating \*\*932 R-1 units\*\*. Engineers must design a sprawling lateral network for KCPL Power and water distribution that accommodates lower-density, high-acreage single-family parcels.

### Trail and Green-Space Integration
Utilize the \*\*80’ Electric Transmission Easement\*\* as a dual-purpose corridor. Direct the placement of \*\*Proposed Multi-Modal Trails\*\* \[SOURCE\_IMAGE\_4\] within this easement and the adjacent \*\*68 acres of Park/Greenspace\*\*. This maximizes the value of restricted-build utility corridors.

### The "So What?" Layer: Tax Stabilization
While the sprawling R-1 residential grid typically presents a higher service-cost-to-tax-yield ratio, this phase is strategically "stabilized" by the simultaneous construction of \*\*2,587,000 SF of Light Industrial\*\* space. This balanced mix satisfies the "Tax Stabilization" requirements of Policy Statement 4, ensuring the residential expansion does not become a fiscal drain on the City.

## 4. Phase 3: Urban Core and Town Center Completion (186 Acres)

Phase 3 is the development’s culmination: the high-density \*\*Town Center (88 acres)\*\* and \*\*Mixed-Use (69 acres)\*\* zones. The infrastructure logic here shifts to vertical density and redundancy.

### High-Density Utility Logic
\*   \*\*Energy Density:\*\* The viability of \*\*1,779 residential units\*\* and \*\*817,000 SF of Retail\*\* in this compact area is entirely dependent on the \*\*80’ Electric Transmission Easement\*\* identified in the baseline. This existing high-voltage asset is the reason the Town Center can sustain its projected density without massive off-site substation upgrades.
\*   \*\*Hydraulic Looping:\*\* Civil teams must complete sophisticated loop systems to ensure fire-flow and water pressure redundancy for the \*\*146,000 SF Medical/Office\*\* and high-rise residential components.

### Public Benefit & Livability
Phase 3 fulfills the Economic Development Vision of creating a "unique downtown" with "high-quality jobs." Infrastructure here must differentiate between private laterals and "extraordinary" public multimodal connections that enhance "accessibility to needs and services"—a key metric for City Council approval of high-density incentives.

## 5. Policy Compliance and Incentive Integration Roadmap

Technical execution is irrelevant without regulatory compliance. Every foot of utility extension must pass the \*\*"But For" test\*\* and adhere to the \*\*Reimbursement Ratio\*\* historically approved by the Council.

### Incentive Tool Matrix
| Utility / Project Type | Recommended Tool | Infrastructure Use Case | Qualification Threshold |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Large-Scale Public Backbone\*\* | \*\*TIF\*\* | \*\*Roads, Sewer, Regional Lift Station\*\* | $5M+ New Investment; 25% Cap |
| \*\*District-Level Utilities\*\* | \*\*CID / TDD\*\* | \*\*Internal Public Streets & Stormwater\*\* | 1-cent Sales Tax / Assessment |
| \*\*Industrial Distribution\*\* | \*\*Chapter 100\*\* | \*\*Industrial Lateral Connections\*\* | 50% Abatement for 10 Years |
| \*\*Commercial Core\*\* | \*\*Chapter 353\*\* | \*\*Mixed-Use Infrastructure\*\* | 50% Abatement for 10 Years |

### Strategic Outcomes Checklist (Infrastructure KPIs)
To secure the incentives above, civil engineers must provide quantifiable proof of the following:
1.  \*\*Blight Remediation:\*\* Quantifiable reduction of undesirable conditions as verified by Policy Statement 3.
2.  \*\*Public Infrastructure Expansion:\*\* Documentation of how the Lift Station and trunk lines create new economic development areas (Policy 11).
3.  \*\*Sustainable Development:\*\* Verified incorporation of green stormwater facilities or energy-saving practices (Policy 5).

### Final Directive to Project Managers
Per \*\*Policy 6\*\*, the City Council \*\*will not\*\* grant incentives for projects that have already received building or infrastructure permits. Project managers must ensure that "Intent to Seek Incentives" is filed before any Preliminary Development Plan action is taken.
