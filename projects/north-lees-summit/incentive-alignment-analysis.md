# Incentive Alignment Analysis: The Land Reserve North Lee’s Summit Project

## 1. Project Overview and Strategic Context

The Land Reserve project is a transformative, 1,036-acre multi-phase development poised to redefine the economic landscape of North Lee’s Summit. Given the scale of this initiative—spanning three phases and over 1,000 acres—alignment with the Lee’s Summit Economic Development Policy is a strategic imperative. For the City, incentives are not mere subsidies but essential fiscal tools used to manage sustainable growth and ensure that large-scale private investments yield a high public return on investment (ROI). 

A critical factor in this project’s history is that approximately 1,100 acres in this targeted area were previously held by Property Reserve, Inc., remaining largely fallow. The current proposal represents the first viable path toward activating this land, which has historically faced development barriers due to undesirable land conditions and challenging topography.

The following data illustrates the project’s immense scope:

### Strategic Location Alignment
The project is situated entirely within the \*\*I-470 North Targeted Planning Area\*\*, which the City defines as "largely undeveloped" and a priority for expansion. Current municipal policy explicitly notes that development here requires "significant sewer and traffic improvements." The Land Reserve project directly addresses these prerequisites, providing the necessary remediation for "undesirable land conditions" that have historically hindered growth in this corridor.

## 2. Alignment with Economic Development Vision and Targeted Outcomes

The City of Lee’s Summit utilizes "Targeted Outcomes" to evaluate whether a project warrants the deployment of public financial tools. These outcomes ensure that development enhances the City’s fiscal sustainability and community livability.

The Land Reserve project demonstrates robust alignment with the following outcomes:

\*   \*\*Infrastructure Development:\*\* The project provides for the extension of high-cost public utilities, including \*\*Sanitary Gravity Mains, Sanitary Force Mains, and a Lift Station\*\*, alongside a multi-modal trail network. \*\*So What?\*\* These improvements create the infrastructure backbone for an entire quadrant of the City, converting underserved land into high-value economic zones.
\*   \*\*Business Attraction (Targeted Businesses):\*\* The project allocates 3.67M SF for Light Industrial and 302k SF for Medical/Office use. \*\*So What?\*\* This massive capacity positions Lee’s Summit as a regional hub for the \*\*science, technology, and manufacturing sectors\*\*, attracting high-skill employment that leverages the local educated workforce.
\*   \*\*Residential Preservation & Enhancement:\*\* By integrating 3,451 residential and 249 senior housing units, the project offers a wide spectrum of housing choices. \*\*So What?\*\* Diverse housing stock improves livability and ensures the City can attract next-generation residents while meeting the needs of an aging population.
\*   \*\*Business Retention/Expansion:\*\* With 1.6M SF of retail and office space, the project creates a fertile environment for local commerce. \*\*So What?\*\* This provides modern, expandable space for professional services, ensuring that growing businesses remain within Lee’s Summit rather than relocating to competing jurisdictions.
\*   \*\*Community Image:\*\* The project dedicates 88 acres to parks and extensive trails, exceeding Unified Development Ordinance (UDO) requirements. \*\*So What?\*\* High-quality, well-planned green spaces reinforce the City's image as a vibrant, premier destination for both residents and corporate tenants.

## 3. Financial Incentive Tool Applicability Matrix

The City maintains a philosophy of being "accommodating to targeted businesses," provided they meet minimum investment thresholds. The following matrix evaluates the Land Reserve project's scale against available municipal tools:

| Incentive Tool | Minimum Investment | Eligibility Status | Strategic Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| \*\*Tax Increment Financing (TIF)\*\* | $5M New / $3M Existing | \*\*Highly Eligible\*\* | Reimburses up to 25% of total private development costs. |
| \*\*Special Districts (CID/TDD)\*\* | Public Improvement Req. | \*\*Highly Eligible\*\* | Funds traffic and trail improvements via up to 1 cent tax. |
| \*\*Chapter 100/353 Bonds\*\* | $5M New / $3M Existing | \*\*Highly Eligible\*\* | Standard 50% abatement for 10 years for targeted sectors. |
| \*\*LCRA\*\* | $500,000 | \*\*Eligible\*\* | 50% - 10-year abatement; useful for specific phases. |
| \*\*Sales Tax Reimbursement\*\* | $250,000 | \*\*Highly Eligible\*\* | Addresses 1.6M SF retail via up to 1/2 cent reimbursement. |

### Analytical Assessment
\*   \*\*Tax Increment Financing (TIF):\*\* The project’s multi-phase scale clears the $5M threshold. Per policy, the "reimbursement ratio" is calculated against the \*\*total private development cost\*\*. Given the significant grading and utility requirements of the site, TIF is the primary mechanism for bridging the "but-for" gap.
\*   \*\*Special Districts (CID/TDD):\*\* These tools are critical for addressing the "significant sewer and traffic improvements" identified in the I-470 North Targeted Planning Area description. They allow for the self-funding of public infrastructure without impacting the City’s General Fund.
\*   \*\*Abatements (Chapter 100/353/LCRA):\*\* For the industrial and medical components, these tools offer a predictable \*\*50% abatement for 10 years\*\*, a baseline requirement to remain competitive in the regional science and technology hub market.
\*   \*\*Sales Tax Reimbursement:\*\* With 1.6M SF of retail, this tool ($250k threshold) is essential for funding public-facing improvements like internal road networks and signage.

## 4. Deep Dive: Residential and Industrial Criteria Compliance

To satisfy the "But For" test, the developer must prove the project would not be financially feasible without incentives or that the incentives will provide "substantially enhanced features."

### Residential Criteria Compliance
\*   \*\*Blight Clearance:\*\* The developer must document the remediation of the "undesirable land conditions" and topography that historically kept the Property Reserve, Inc. land undeveloped.
\*   \*\*Public Improvements:\*\* Emphasis must be placed on extraordinary off-site improvements, specifically those benefiting the \*\*NE Todd George Pkwy\*\* corridor.
\*   \*\*Tax Redirection Impact:\*\* Because property tax redirection is a high-friction point, the developer must provide detailed fiscal impact analysis for the \*\*Lee’s Summit and Blue Springs School Districts\*\*, which share jurisdiction over the project area.

### Industrial Criteria Compliance
\*   \*\*Job Creation & Retention:\*\* Most favorable consideration is given to projects creating new jobs that are \*\*not relocated from another location in the City\*\*. Average compensation must meet or exceed the Jackson County average.
\*   \*\*Capitalization:\*\* The policy requires that at least \*\*15% of total project costs be funded from company equity\*\* rather than debt. This is a critical threshold for a project of this magnitude.
\*   \*\*Sustainable Development:\*\* The City prioritizes "green development" and energy-saving practices in industrial construction, which should be reflected in the final site plans.

## 5. Implementation Roadmap and Procedural Requirements

Approval is granted case-by-case by the City Council through "judicious review." No incentive is an entitlement, and the City’s General Fund will not be pledged as a guarantee for private debt.

### Mandatory Next Steps
1.  \*\*Preliminary Development Plan Disclosure:\*\* Intent to seek incentives must be disclosed in writing before Council action on the Preliminary Development Plan to avoid disqualification.
2.  \*\*Public vs. Private Differentiation:\*\* Budgets must explicitly bifurcate public improvements (roads, utilities, parks) from private construction costs to determine appropriate reimbursement ratios.
3.  \*\*Third-Party Review:\*\* The developer must fund an independent, third-party analysis of the project’s ROI, cost-benefit ratio, and overall feasibility.
4.  \*\*Taxing Jurisdiction Impact:\*\* A written \*\*Statement of Impact\*\* must be prepared for the Lee’s Summit and Blue Springs School Districts to address tax revenue shifts.
5.  \*\*Annual Evaluation:\*\* Per General Policy #7, incentive disbursements are subject to an \*\*annual check\*\* to ensure the project’s performance remains commensurate with the granted public support.

The Land Reserve North Lee’s Summit project possesses the scale to be the most significant development in the I-470 North Targeted Area’s history. By adhering to these rigorous policy standards, the project can secure a municipal partnership that expands the tax base and preserves the City’s "outstanding quality of life."
