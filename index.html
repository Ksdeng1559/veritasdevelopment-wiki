C-1.7 Co-Sponsorship & Capital Partners

The other side of the stack — equity and sponsor capacity, not repayment.

Everything through C-1.6 sources debt — capital that gets repaid on a schedule regardless of the deal's outcome. This section sources the partners who sit on the equity side of the capital stack: Co-GP, institutional sponsor, JV equity, preferred equity, and capital-advisor relationships that fill a GP co-investment gap, bring balance-sheet and back-office capacity Veritas doesn't have in-house, or place equity from their own investor network. Pulled from the companion Capital Partner Matrix — Co-Sponsors & Strategic Partners Notion database (linked from the same Veritas Capital Markets resource as the lender matrix), supplemented this session with live program-page research for the roles the database doesn't cover yet.

Partner roles

Co-GP

A capital partner invests alongside Veritas at the general-partner level, sharing GP obligations and participating in the promote. Used when a JV's LP requires a GP co-investment Veritas's own balance sheet can't fully fund on its own — the partner's capital counts toward that requirement.

Institutional Sponsor Partner

Furthest along the spectrum from "capital only" toward "co-operator" — a partner with the balance sheet, guarantee capacity, and back-office infrastructure (accounting, legal, compliance, reporting) to stand alongside or occasionally in front of Veritas as sponsor of record on a deal that needs institutional credibility Veritas doesn't yet carry alone.

JV Equity

Traditional LP/JV structure: the partner funds the majority of project equity (typically 80–95%) for a preferred return plus a share of profit above it, while Veritas contributes a smaller co-investment and retains operating control as GP.

Preferred Equity

Capital positioned between senior debt and common equity — repaid after the lender but before Veritas or any common-equity partner sees a distribution. Fills a capital-stack gap (often construction cost growth or a refinance shortfall) without diluting ownership as much as raising new common equity would.

Placement / Capital Advisor

Doesn't provide capital directly — sources and structures debt and/or equity from its own investor network in exchange for a placement fee. Worth engaging when Veritas's own relationship pipeline (below) doesn't reach the right check size or investor type for a specific deal.

C-2.0 Ratio Engine — Property Cash Flow, Not Business Cash Flow

DSCR and Debt Yield replace EBITDA/FCCR as the core tests.

The original framework's Core 5 (DSCR, FCCR, Debt/EBITDA, Current Ratio, LTV) is a business-lending stack. Real estate debt is underwritten off the property's net operating income, not the sponsor's income statement — the sponsor's financials matter as a secondary guarantor test, not the primary repayment source.

DSCR
NOI ÷ Annual Debt Service
Floor: 1.25x perm · 1.20x agency/affordable · test at stressed rate
Debt Yield
NOI ÷ Loan Amount
Floor: 9–10% — protects against cap-rate/refi risk, independent of amortization
LTV
Loan ÷ Appraised Value
Ceiling: 65–70% perm · higher for agency multifamily
LTC
Loan ÷ Total Project Cost
Ceiling: 70–75% construction — this is what your capital-stack "sources = uses" gap tracks
Cap Rate
NOI ÷ Value or Price
Compare going-in vs. exit — spread compression is the #1 stress-test variable
Yield on Cost
Stabilized NOI ÷ Total Dev. Cost
Development-specific — must clear exit cap rate by 100–200 bps to justify the build
Stressed DSCR
NOI ÷ Debt Service at rate +200bps
Underwriting rate, not note rate — the equivalent of the original's "rate sensitivity" test
Sponsor Liquidity
Post-closing liquidity ÷ Loan Amount
Floor: ≥10% of loan, plus 12 months' debt service — the guarantor-strength test

⚠ Risk signals: Stressed DSCR < 1.10 · Debt Yield < 8% · LTV/LTC above segment ceiling with no compensating strength · single-tenant or >30% rollover inside loan term · going-in cap rate inside 50bps of market comps (no cushion).

C-3.0 Loan Structuring Logic

How the mechanics differ by facility type.

Construction

Draw & holdback structure

Funded against a draw schedule tied to % completion, verified by an inspecting engineer report each draw. Retainage held back (typically 5–10%) until substantial completion. Interest reserve funded up front and drawn monthly — not paid out of pocket. Requires a completion guaranty from the sponsor and a cost-to-complete analysis at every draw.

Ties to: OP-M1 monitoring code, HC-07 GC contract hold
Bridge / Mini-Perm

Floating-rate, covenant-light

Underwrites to as-stabilized NOI, not trailing NOI — the gap between today's cash flow and stabilization is the entire risk. Shorter term (2–3 yrs + extension options tied to DSCR/leasing hurdles). Priced off SOFR; often interest-only for the full term with a maturity/refi assumption baked into the exit.

Ties to: OP-R1/OP-R2, HP-03 pre-leasing hold
Permanent / Agency

Amortizing, non-recourse

Fixed-rate, 25–30 year amortization, 10-year typical term with a balloon or defeasance/yield-maintenance prepayment penalty. Non-recourse with standard carve-outs (fraud, waste, environmental, voluntary bankruptcy). Replacement reserves and, for office/retail, a TI/LC reserve are escrowed monthly.

Ties to: OP-C5 non-recourse, OP-M4 covenant testing

Multi-layer stack: senior debt (bank/agency) + mezzanine or preferred equity (gap financing) + sponsor equity, governed by an intercreditor agreement that sets standstill, cure, and buy-out rights between senior and mezz. This is the direct US equivalent of the original framework's "A lender + B + private" layering — the difference is the intercreditor paper, which PPSA-style GSA layering doesn't require in the same form.

C-7.0 Decision Engine

The automatable thresholds behind the recommendation.

Approve

  • Stressed DSCR ≥ 1.25x (1.20x agency/affordable)
  • Debt Yield ≥ 9%
  • LTV / LTC within segment ceiling
  • Sponsor liquidity ≥ 10% of loan + 12 mo. debt service
  • Title, environmental, and zoning clear or resolvable pre-close

Refer

  • Stressed DSCR 1.10x – 1.25x
  • Debt Yield 7% – 9%
  • LTV/LTC above ceiling but within secondary-market range
  • Rollover, completion, or collateral gap needing a mitigant
Cure path: add reserve, reduce proceeds, require completion or carve-out guaranty, or layer mezz/pref to right-size the senior loan.

Decline

  • Stressed DSCR < 1.10x with no credible repayment source
  • LTV/LTC exceeds policy with no compensating strength
  • Unresolved environmental REC with no remediation path
  • Sponsor character, fraud, or title defect issue

This is Phase B of what the Veritas Capital prototype already started.

Phase B turns that into a decision engine: the same property/deal inputs already being entered per-project run through the ratio engine, generate the hold-code conditions list automatically, and score-match against the live Lender Matrix Pro Notion database (C-1.5) by stage instead of requiring a manual read of each lender's fit.

C-9.0 Where This Framework Comes From

Public, defensible, US-specific — not a guess at a lender's internal codes.

Interagency CRE Concentration Guidance

OCC / Federal Reserve / FDIC (2006, still governing): flags CRE concentration risk management when construction+land loans exceed 100% of capital, or total CRE exceeds 300% — the closest US analog to OSFI's principle-based approach.

Agency multifamily guides

Fannie Mae Multifamily and Freddie Mac Multifamily seller/servicer guides publish DSCR/LTV underwriting grids by product — the public source for the 1.25x/1.20x DSCR and LTV bands used above.

ASTM E1527 (Phase I ESA)

The industry-standard environmental due-diligence protocol referenced by every institutional lender's environmental hold code.

ALTA/NSPS survey & UCC Article 9

Title/survey standard and the US fixture-filing statute that replaces PPSA registration in this framework.

What's institution-specific vs. universal: exact internal hold-code and op-code labels at any specific bank, agency shop, or debt fund are proprietary and not publicly standardized — the same caveat that applied to the original Canadian framework applies here. What's built above is the same kind of institution-agnostic system: close enough to how real lenders structure and monitor CRE debt to be useful for pre-qualifying a deal, prepping a submission package, and running the Lender Matrix — not a claim to have reverse-engineered any specific lender's actual codes. Verify every threshold against the specific term sheet before relying on it, and route security documents through counsel before execution.
